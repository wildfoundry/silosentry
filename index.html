See the company running Start from the devices

The point

Not a PagerDuty clone. Not a Geckoboard wall. Not “SSH in the cloud.”

Those tools solve slices. An IoT product company needs the slices attached to the same device records — provision → presence → remote access → monitoring → incidents → remediations → commercial lifecycle — without duct-taping five vendors together.

Tool salad

A status board here, an on-call pager there, a VPN spreadsheet for shells, a separate ticket for RMA. The device is the missing primary key — every escalation starts with “which box?” and ends in Slack archaeology.

Dataplicity as the spine

The device is the unit of work. Dashboards watch fleets that already exist in the account. Incidents page humans about those same devices. Fleet jobs and terminals act on them. Licences, warranty, and RMA stay on the same org — because shipping hardware didn’t stop at install.

After they ship

Everything starts with devices you can still reach.

SiloSentry’s cloud sells bin telemetry. Dataplicity is how support keeps the Linux gateways operable — outbound agent, no inbound ports, one inventory for every elevator.

  1. Install outbound First-boot one-liner. Works behind farm routers and LTE.
  2. It shows up Org inventory by site and SKU — not an IP someone texted you.
  3. Reach it Wormhole for the local UI. Terminal when a process is wedged.
  4. Act on many Fleet jobs, Pulse drift, alerts — still keyed to those devices.

The inventory

This is the fleet the company supports

Probe GWs in bins. Site Gateways at the truck dump. Same list whether the co-op is in Grand Forks or Amarillo — the starting point for dashboards, pages, and remediations.

Break-glass

Still one record when a human has to finish it

Incidents and jobs don’t replace hands-on access — they route you to the right gateway. Terminal and Wormhole live on that device, not in a separate VPN world.

Run the company

Ops center, incidents, progression, remediations — on the same spine.

This is what “using Dataplicity to manage devices after they ship” looks like day to day at SiloSentry — not a feature checklist, a company shape.

Ops center dashboards

A wall that watches your fleets — not a generic KPI board

SiloSentry’s NOC layout is built from the same org: fleet map, gateway check-ins, customer-cloud monitors, on-call. It’s Geckoboard-shaped only at a glance — the tiles are Dataplicity devices and monitors, not pasted screenshots from elsewhere.

Look for: “Ops center — Plains elevators”, check-in heartbeats, on-call strip.

Incident response

Page people about devices and services that already live here

Tower 2’s probe-daemon, a Red River offline cohort, customer-cloud latency — ack, own, escalate, publish status. This isn’t “we also bolted on a pager.” The incident names the resource your fleet already tracks.

Look for: Critical Tower 2 incident, Warning on app.silosentry.com, Acknowledge actions.

On-call & escalation

Who gets woken — and for what severity

Escalation paths and mobile Critical Alerts sit next to the incidents, not in a separate vendor login. The company can answer “who owns harvest weekend?” without leaving Dataplicity.

Fleet progression

Online isn’t the same as “still normal”

Pulse learns what Probe GW usually looks like, then shows who drifted — missing probe-daemon, disk climbing — over time. That’s lifecycle ops: the fleet changes, and you can see it.

Remediation at scale

One guarded command across the set

Harvest readiness, log vacuums, targeted restarts — fleet jobs record what ran on which gateway. The same spine that paged you also carries the fix.

Put together

One SiloSentry day on Dataplicity.

If someone asks how you’d run your IoT company after devices ship, walk them through this.

Morning

Ops overview + wall + Pulse

Scan attention. Glance the Plains elevators dashboard. Open Probe GW Pulse — coverage full, one tower drifting.

Midday

Incidents → fleet job

Ack the Tower 2 page. Run a restart (or harvest readiness) as a fleet job. Watch per-device results before anyone drives out.

Afternoon

Break-glass + backlog

Whatever didn’t clear gets terminal or Wormhole on that device record. Alerts keep the rest from vanishing into Slack.

Ongoing

Commercial lifecycle stays attached

Customers, licence tiers, warranty, RMA — still in the sidebar. The hardware didn’t leave Dataplicity when it left the factory; neither did the business process around it.
